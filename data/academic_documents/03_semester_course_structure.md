# Semester Course Structure

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.

## How the structure is organized

The B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering program has eight semesters over four years. Each semester contributes 20 credits in this compact synthetic design. Academic year 2026-2027 serves several cohorts at once: Semesters 1, 3, 5 and 7 are in the odd term; Semesters 2, 4, 6 and 8 are in the even term. A student's own progression remains sequential even though the institution teaches several semester numbers concurrently.

The tables emphasize delivery and course identity. A theory course and its laboratory are separately assessed courses, not two names for one subject. Projects and seminars use practical delivery without becoming laboratory courses. The word elective records the fixed choice for this cohort; it does not authorize substitution with an unlisted course.

## Semesters 5, 6 and 7: the language and AI pathway

Machine Learning appears in Semester 5 as HCE-CSE502 with four credits. Artificial Intelligence is a different four-credit course in that semester. The Machine Learning Laboratory is HCE-CSE505 with two credits and takes the theory course as a same-semester corequisite. The semester also includes Theory of Computation, Data Engineering and Software Engineering Project, so predictive learning sits within a broader computing curriculum.

Natural Language Processing is HCE-CSE603 in Semester 6 with four credits. Its laboratory, HCE-CSE605, contributes two credits. Information Retrieval is adjacent in the same semester, creating a connection between language representation and ranking evidence. The NLP course requires prior Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning; it is not placed after Deep Learning in the declared sequence.

Deep Learning is HCE-CSE701 in Semester 7 with four credits. The two-credit Deep Learning Laboratory is HCE-CSE705. Machine Learning and Linear Algebra for Computing provide its prerequisites. Computer Vision, Information Security, Distributed Systems and Capstone Project I complete this semester's structure.

## Using related records

Consult Course Prerequisites for dependencies and Faculty Course Information for named assignments. The calendar defines term dates and examination windows; this register does not invent individual exam dates. Syllabi explain learning content. The distinction between a semester register and a syllabus prevents a table of course names from being treated as a complete account of what every subject teaches.

When comparing a lab with its theory subject, retain both codes in notes and searches. Separate identifiers make it possible to retrieve the correct course's credit count and evaluation mode without conflating related academic records.
