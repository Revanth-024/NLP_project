# Laboratory Guidelines

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.


## Purpose and course identity

Laboratories connect an academic method with observable inputs, procedures and results. They are separately coded courses in the synthetic curriculum, even when they accompany a theory subject. The related theory course is studied concurrently as a corequisite. Earlier-course preparation is listed separately in Course Prerequisites; the two relationships should not be merged.

The AI pathway includes Machine Learning Laboratory (HCE-CSE505) in Semester 5, Natural Language Processing Laboratory (HCE-CSE605) in Semester 6, and Deep Learning Laboratory (HCE-CSE705) in Semester 7. Each carries two credits and is linked to its corresponding four-credit theory course. These examples do not imply that all other courses have an associated laboratory or that every laboratory has a named faculty assignment in the dataset.

## Participation and preparation

Laboratory attendance uses the same 75% per-course minimum as the general policy. There is no special lab threshold. Participation is measured against held scheduled sessions without rounding the percentage upward. Continuous work and a successful demonstration do not waive an attendance shortage. The attendance document explains corrections, approved absence and re-enrollment after shortage.

An experiment should make its input and method clear enough that the learner can explain what was observed. Reading a theory description is not the same as documenting a practical observation. These guidelines describe the common record and demonstration requirements; they do not prescribe a numbered experiment list or silently introduce tools and downloads that the workshop has not yet implemented.

## Record submission

Submit a dated record for each assigned experiment, including inputs, method, observations and conclusion, by the term's records milestone.

The record connects a result with the input and method that produced it. An observation may be surprising or incomplete; explain it rather than replacing it with an unsupported expected answer. A conclusion should state what the particular experiment supports, without claiming that one successful example establishes universal performance. The calendar's term milestone supplies the due date, so this document does not invent a separate submission date.

## Experiment completion and demonstration

Complete every assigned experiment before the end-semester practical; the experiment list will be defined in later laboratory documents.

Demonstrate working results and explain the method during the practical viva; project labs also demonstrate their final artifact.

The demonstration asks the student to connect an artifact with its procedure and results. A record provides evidence of practical work, while the viva provides an opportunity to explain choices and limitations. Projects that use laboratory delivery also demonstrate their final artifact. Course-specific experiment lists will be introduced later; none are invented in these common guidelines.

## Evaluation and incomplete work

Laboratory evaluation uses the shared 100-mark scheme: 40 internal marks and 60 end-semester marks. Internal assessment comprises a 20-mark midterm practical and 20 marks of continuous records and experiments. End-semester assessment uses practical demonstration and viva. Passing requires at least 50 total marks and at least 24 end-semester marks, alongside attendance eligibility. No separate internal minimum applies.

Incomplete records or experiments affect continuous coursework marks; they do not introduce a separate examination eligibility threshold.

This distinction avoids turning a coursework issue into a new attendance exception or a different examination eligibility policy. The practical requirements still matter because they form evidence for assessment, even though the general eligibility rule remains unchanged.

## Safety, discipline and responsible work

Use only instructor-approved datasets and software. Do not share passwords or include personal data in experiments. Handle equipment responsibly and report faults. Acknowledge collaboration while submitting your own explanation of results. These common expectations support a safe, explainable practical environment without requiring a commercial platform or specifying a real laboratory address.

Refer to the academic calendar for laboratory examination windows and record milestones. No individual laboratory appointment is fixed in the contract. When a question asks for a particular practical date, report the approved window and the absence of a more specific schedule rather than guessing one.
