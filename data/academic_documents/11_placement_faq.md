# Placement FAQ

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.


## What does placement support cover?

This FAQ describes institution-level preparation and recruitment registration for the fictional B.Tech CSE cohort. It contains no employer-specific claims, company eligibility rules, salary figures or placement statistics. Placement support creates opportunities to prepare and present academic work; it is not evidence that a particular employer will recruit from the institution.

## Who may join preparation activities?

Students in Semesters 5 through 8 may register for placement preparation, including students with pending courses.

Preparation and recruitment eligibility are different decisions. A student with an unfinished course can still work on a resume or practise explaining a technical project. Access to those activities does not mean that the same student satisfies the later registration conditions. The distinction should be retained when summarizing this FAQ or answering a short question about a pending course.

## Who is eligible for recruitment registration?

Recruitment registration is for students in Semesters 7 and 8. The minimum CGPA is 6.0, and the maximum permitted number of pending courses is zero. Required materials are an updated resume, a completed academic record and completed preparation orientation. These are the institution's synthetic registration conditions; they do not imply that an external employer has accepted a student.

The CGPA refers to the credit-weighted grade record described in Examination Regulations. It is not an attendance percentage and should not be replaced by a percentage calculated from one examination. The pending-course condition is also separate from the CGPA threshold. Satisfying one condition does not cancel another, and participation in a preparation activity does not waive either one.

## What support is available?

The listed support consists of a resume clinic, aptitude practice and a mock technical interview. A resume clinic helps a student present their preparation and project work clearly. Aptitude practice provides a setting for discussing problem-solving approaches. A mock technical interview offers an opportunity to explain technical choices. No compulsory number of practice sessions or dated activity schedule is specified in the contract.

Students can use their academic work to explain what they studied and built. A Machine Learning experiment may illustrate evaluation choices; an NLP exercise may illustrate language representations; a capstone artifact may illustrate the relationship between a method and its evidence. These are examples of how to discuss work, not additional recruitment qualifications or a guarantee that one particular project is required.

## Who is the contact role?

The contact role is the Department placement coordinator. No personal name, email address, telephone number or office location is defined. Faculty Course Information lists course instructors, but an instructor's name must not be repurposed as a placement contact without a canonical assignment. Keeping the role separate avoids creating fictional contact details that look like real institutional information.

## Does registration guarantee an outcome?

Preparation or eligibility does not guarantee an interview, internship or job.

Eligibility means that the institution's defined registration requirements have been met. It is not an offer, an employer shortlist or a promise of salary. This dataset deliberately avoids time-sensitive employer information so students can practise retrieval without interpreting invented recruitment claims as current real-world opportunities.

## How should an unresolved case be answered?

Use the explicit distinction between preparation access and recruitment registration. If a question asks about a pending course, preparation remains accessible in the defined semesters, while recruitment requires zero pending courses. If it asks for employer policy, a recruitment deadline or a contact address, explain that those facts are not specified. Do not infer a missing rule from unrelated attendance or examination conditions.
