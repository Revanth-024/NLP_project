# Student Academic Regulations

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.


## Scope and program identity

These regulations describe the synthetic B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering program for academic year 2026-2027 in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering. The institution is situated in the invented teaching location of Velmora, Teralin Learning District. Its degree duration is 4 years across 8 semesters. The compact curriculum is an educational design and does not claim accreditation or equivalence to a real institution's regulations.

The curriculum identifies courses by stable codes rather than informal nicknames. A course's semester indicates its normal place in the program. Credits express its contribution to the academic record; marks express performance in an assessment. A laboratory is a separate course with its own code and credits, even when it is closely connected to a theory subject. Listed electives are the fixed choices of this synthetic cohort, not a menu of additional unlisted subjects.

## Registration and academic sequence

Students should consult the semester course structure alongside the prerequisite register when preparing their study plan. Prerequisites describe earlier courses that supply necessary foundations. Corequisites identify theory subjects studied in the same semester as their associated laboratories. Treating a corequisite as an earlier prerequisite would misrepresent the intended sequence. The register supplies the complete declared relationships; an omitted relationship must not be invented from a similarity between course names.

Different cohorts attend odd semesters together and even semesters together. The academic calendar therefore contains two terms within one academic year, rather than eight consecutive teaching periods. It specifies registration milestones and examination windows. Only the NLP course has a separately fixed examination date. Dates for other individual courses, or future reassessments, should not be inferred from the first day of an examination window.

## Participation and examination eligibility

The minimum attendance is 75% in each enrolled course. It applies to theory, laboratories, projects and seminars separately. Attendance is the proportion of held scheduled sessions that the student attended, evaluated without rounding the percentage upward. A strong attendance record in one course does not cancel a shortage in another. Students with insufficient participation are ineligible for the affected course's end-semester evaluation.

There is no condonation route in this synthetic policy. Approved leave does not automatically count as classroom participation. A verified recording error may be corrected because it changes the underlying record, not the required threshold. A shortage requires re-enrollment at the next offering. Internal assessment marks cannot waive attendance eligibility, and completed laboratory records do not create an exception to the general rule.

## Assessment and passing

The course has 100 total marks: 40 internal assessment marks and 60 end-semester marks. Passing requires at least 50 total marks and at least 24 end-semester marks. Both conditions apply together; there is no separate internal minimum. Attendance eligibility remains a separate requirement.

Internal assessment consists of a 20-mark midterm assessment and 20 marks of continuous coursework. Written examinations support theory courses, while practical demonstrations and viva support laboratory assessment. Projects and seminars use progress review, portfolio work and a final demonstration or viva. These different modes use the same numerical scheme; they do not introduce alternative passing totals.

An eligible student who fails either pass condition may take reassessment at the next announced opportunity. The present calendar does not specify that date. Attendance ineligibility is a different situation from an eligible student's unsuccessful examination performance, and its recovery rule is re-enrollment rather than an assumed reassessment entitlement.

## Grades and supporting documents

Absolute grade bands are applied only after the pass requirements are satisfied. Recorded final marks are whole numbers. An F grade carries zero grade points. Attendance ineligibility is recorded as NE and withholds the semester GPA until the course is completed. The GPA is a credit-weighted mean; credits and grade points therefore have distinct roles.

For a detailed participation decision, consult the Attendance Policy. For grade bands and assessment conditions, consult Examination Regulations. For experiment records and demonstrations, use Laboratory Guidelines. The calendar supplies dates, while syllabi supply course content. Together these documents describe one academic world without making every document reproduce every rule in full.
