## Program overview

B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering (B.Tech CSE) spans four years and eight semesters in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering. The curriculum below contains 47 courses totaling 160 credits. It describes the fixed synthetic cohort for 2026-2027; it is not a real accreditation document. Theory subjects, laboratories, seminars, projects and fixed electives have distinct roles but contribute to the same program.

## Selected course profiles

**Computer Networks (HCE-CSE402):** Protocols and layered architectures that enable digital devices to exchange data over connected systems.

**Machine Learning (HCE-CSE502):** Data-driven methods that learn patterns from examples to make predictions on unseen observations.

**Natural Language Processing (HCE-CSE603):** Computational methods for processing, understanding and generating human language through symbolic, statistical and contextual representations.

**Deep Learning (HCE-CSE701):** Multilayer neural methods for learning hierarchical representations from data, building on Machine Learning.

## Reading the curriculum

The table gives placement and workload, not the complete syllabus or a list of registration exceptions. Detailed ML, NLP and Deep Learning content appears in the respective syllabus documents. The prerequisite register distinguishes earlier preparation from same-semester theory/lab pairing. An assigned faculty member appears only where the fact contract provides one; course availability does not imply that the dataset names every instructor.

The shared storyline runs from numerical preparation through predictive learning to language representations and deeper neural models. Related course names should not be interpreted as identical objectives. Information Retrieval studies relevance over collections, while NLP studies language representations and processing. Computer Networks concerns communication between devices, whereas language courses concern representations of human communication. These contrasts make the curriculum useful for both direct lookup and meaning-based discovery.
