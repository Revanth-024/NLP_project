# Attendance Policy

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.


## Minimum attendance and scope

Students must maintain at least 75% attendance in each enrolled course. The requirement applies separately to theory courses, laboratories, projects and seminars. Classroom participation in one subject cannot be transferred to another subject's attendance record. There is one institutional threshold; laboratory participation does not use an additional or reduced percentage.

Attendance eligibility refers to participation in scheduled sessions that were actually held. It is not a score on an assignment and it is not the total number of credits registered. When discussing a shortage, identify the affected course and the underlying session record. A general statement that a student attends college regularly is insufficient to establish eligibility in every subject.

## Calculating the participation record

The attendance percentage is the number of attended scheduled sessions divided by the number of held scheduled sessions, multiplied by 100. Compare the unrounded result with the minimum. Rounding a lower value upward for display does not make it eligible. The relevant denominator is held sessions, not all dates printed in a calendar or an assumed total from a different course.

For an illustrative arithmetic example, attending 15 of 20 held sessions gives 75% and reaches the threshold. Attending 14 of those same 20 sessions does not. These counts explain the rule; they are not a mandated number of sessions for a semester. Course timetables and total session counts are not specified in the fact contract.

## Shortage and end-semester evaluation

A percentage below the minimum is an attendance shortage. The student is ineligible for that course's end-semester evaluation. This includes a written examination, laboratory practical, project demonstration or seminar evaluation according to the course's delivery mode. Good performance in continuous coursework does not cancel a shortage. Internal marks alone cannot waive the participation requirement.

Eligibility is distinct from passing. Reaching the attendance minimum permits end-semester evaluation under the policy; it does not establish that a student will meet the examination pass conditions. Conversely, an estimated high examination score does not establish eligibility before the participation rule is satisfied. Examination Regulations explain the marks needed to pass after eligibility is established.

## Leave, corrections and condonation

No condonation is available in this synthetic policy. Approved leave does not automatically count as an attended session. Approval recognizes an absence but does not change the meaning of attendance or create a second percentage for exceptional cases. The policy does not define discretionary reductions, paid exemptions or separate attendance bands.

A student may request correction of an attendance recording error. A verified correction changes the record of what happened; it does not relax the threshold. The distinction matters when interpreting a claim that an attendance issue was resolved: correcting an incorrectly recorded session is different from approving a genuine shortage. The contract does not specify an application deadline or a named office for corrections, so neither should be inferred from this document.

## Recovery and linked guidance

An attendance shortage requires re-enrollment in the affected course at its next offering and compliance with the same rule. A reassessment opportunity for an eligible student who failed an examination is a different recovery path. The present policy does not announce the next course offering date or allow one recovery path to substitute for the other.

Laboratory Guidelines use this same attendance rule and explain records, experiments and demonstrations. Student Academic Regulations summarize how participation connects with evaluation. The calendar supplies teaching and examination periods but cannot resolve a personal attendance record. These related documents support the policy without adding exceptions to it.
