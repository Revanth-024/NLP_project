# Examination Regulations

**Synthetic educational data created for NLP workshop purposes.**

Hindu College of Engineering is a completely fictional educational institution created only for this workshop.


## Shared assessment scheme

Every course uses one numerical evaluation structure. Internal assessment contributes 40 marks and end-semester evaluation contributes 60 marks, making 100 marks in total. The internal allocation contains 20 marks for midterm assessment and 20 marks for continuous coursework. The same weights apply to theory, laboratory, project and seminar courses even though the evidence used to assess them differs.

Marks and credits must not be confused. Credits describe how a course contributes to the program and the credit-weighted GPA. Assessment marks describe performance within that course. A two-credit laboratory still uses the shared 100-mark scheme; it is not evaluated out of a smaller total simply because it has fewer credits than its related theory course.

## Eligibility and passing requirements

End-semester eligibility follows Attendance Policy: at least 75% attendance in the affected course. The policy specifies no other examination eligibility conditions. Laboratory records and experiment completion contribute to continuous coursework, rather than introducing an additional eligibility threshold. Attendance shortage is recorded as NE; good internal marks do not override it.

To pass, a student must obtain at least 50 total marks and at least 24 end-semester marks. Both conditions must hold. There is no separate internal minimum. A combined total that reaches the pass total cannot compensate for an end-semester score below its required minimum. Likewise, reaching the end-semester minimum does not remove the overall-total condition.

For an arithmetic illustration, 30 internal marks and 24 end-semester marks give a total of 54 and meet both numerical conditions. A combination of 40 internal and 20 end-semester marks reaches a total of 60 but fails the end-semester condition. A combination of 20 internal and 24 end-semester marks meets the component minimum but falls below the required overall total. These examples assume attendance eligibility; they do not replace it.

## Evidence used in different course types

Theory courses use a written midterm, coursework and a written end-semester examination. Laboratories use a midterm practical, continuous records and experiments, and an end-semester practical demonstration with viva. Projects and seminars use progress review and a continuous portfolio, followed by a final demonstration or viva. These delivery-specific modes explain how a learner presents work; they do not create different mark totals or pass conditions.

The NLP previous-paper collection contains four synthetic 60-mark end-semester papers. Each has twelve five-mark questions with all questions compulsory. Those paper marks represent the end-semester component only, not an extra assessment added to the 100-mark course total. The papers do not announce examination duration or historical sitting dates.

## Absolute grading

Apply the following bands only after both numerical pass requirements are met:

| Total marks | Grade | Grade points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–100 | O | 10 |
| 80–89 | A+ | 9 |
| 70–79 | A | 8 |
| 60–69 | B+ | 7 |
| 50–59 | B | 6 |

Recorded final marks are whole numbers. Failure carries grade F and zero grade points. An ineligible course has status NE and withholds the semester GPA until the course is completed. GPA is the credit-weighted mean of grade points, with F contributing zero. A high combined mark cannot be assigned a passing grade when the end-semester pass condition has failed.

## Recovery and schedules

An eligible student who fails either pass requirement may take reassessment at the next announced opportunity. No reassessment date is defined in the present academic calendar. Attendance shortage requires re-enrollment at the next offering under the participation policy, rather than an assumed reassessment entitlement.

Use the calendar for approved windows and the specifically fixed NLP examination date. Other individual course dates remain unspecified. Course syllabi describe content, while these regulations describe the shared evaluation structure. A response involving both content and timing should consult both kinds of record.
